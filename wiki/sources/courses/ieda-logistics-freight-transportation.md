## 概述

IEDA 2410「物流與貨運運輸作業」是香港科技大學 IEDA 學系的二年級必修課，由齊向通教授於 2022 年秋季主講。課程採用 Ghiani、Laporte 及 Musmanno 合著的《Introduction to Logistics Systems Management》（第 2 版，Wiley，2013）為參考教材，系統涵蓋航空貨運、海運（佔全球貿易量約 80%）及陸運三大運輸模式的特性、作業結構與成本比較，並深入介紹倉儲設計（越庫作業 cross-docking、ABC 存放策略）、第三方物流（3PL/4PL）及運輸經濟學（規模經濟、距離衰減、樞紐輻射網絡）。課程以香港及珠三角的港口與跨境物流為具體案例，並在後半段探討電商物流、自動化最後一哩及冷鏈等新興議題。評核方式為作業（20%）、小組專案報告（30%）及三次隨堂測驗（共 50%），要求學生在第 13 週進行小組課堂展示，是銜接 IEDA 3460（需求供應分析）與 IEDA 3410（路徑與車隊管理）的重要基礎課程。

## Course Overview

**IEDA 2410: Logistics and Freight Transportation Operations** is a required IEDA course taught by Prof. Xiangtong Qi (Fall 2022). It covers the operational and economic structure of freight logistics across all major transport modes. The course uses Ghiani, Laporte & Musmanno's *Introduction to Logistics Systems Management* (2nd ed., Wiley, 2013) as a reference text, supplemented by Canvas lecture notes.

## Key Topics

### Intermodalism and Globalization
Modern freight rarely travels by a single mode end-to-end. **Intermodal transportation** chains air, sea, and land legs using standardized containers (ISO 20-ft and 40-ft TEUs). Globalization has extended supply chains across continents, making seamless mode-switching a competitive necessity. Connects to [[wiki/concepts/supply-chain-management|Supply Chain Management]].

### Air Transportation
Air cargo systems: freighter aircraft vs. belly cargo on passenger flights, hub-and-spoke vs. point-to-point routing, airway bills, freight forwarders, customs clearance. Key trade-offs: speed vs. cost — air is 4–5x more expensive than ocean per kg but dramatically faster.

### Water Transportation
Ocean shipping dominates global trade by volume (~80% of world trade by tonnage). Topics include vessel types (container ships, bulk carriers, tankers), port operations, liner services vs. tramp shipping, freight rates (spot vs. contract), and maritime conventions (Incoterms). The **Port of Hong Kong** and **Port of Shenzhen** serve as regional case studies.

### Land Transportation
Trucking, rail freight, and last-mile delivery. Topics include less-than-truckload (LTL) vs. full truckload (FTL), rail intermodal, drayage, and driver regulations. Hong Kong's dense road network and cross-border trucking to mainland China are key contexts.

### Warehousing and Storage
Warehouse design (layout, slotting, picking systems), cross-docking (goods flow through without storage), distribution center (DC) vs. fulfilment center operations, inventory positioning decisions. Connects to [[wiki/concepts/logistics-and-transportation|Logistics and Transportation]].

### Third Party Logistics (3PL)
Outsourcing logistics functions to specialist providers. **1PL** = in-house; **2PL** = asset-based carriers; **3PL** = full logistics services (warehousing + transport + customs); **4PL** = supply chain integrators managing multiple 3PLs. Trade-offs: cost vs. control vs. flexibility.

### Transportation Economics
Economies of scale in transport (larger ships/planes have lower per-unit cost), **distance decay** (cost per km falls at longer distances), commodity bundling, and freight rate structures. Shipper vs. carrier perspectives on rate negotiation. Connects to [[wiki/concepts/logistics-and-transportation|Logistics and Transportation]].

### New Developments in Logistics
E-commerce logistics, last-mile delivery innovation (drones, autonomous vehicles, crowd-sourced delivery), cold chain logistics, and platform-based freight matching (Uber Freight, Lalamove). Connects to [[wiki/concepts/ecommerce-models|E-commerce Models]].

## Practical Takeaways

- The **mode selection decision** (air vs. sea vs. land) is fundamentally a speed/cost/reliability optimization — understanding each mode's cost structure is essential.
- **3PL relationships** involve principal-agent dynamics; service level agreements (SLAs) must carefully specify KPIs to avoid moral hazard.
- **Cross-docking** eliminates storage cost but requires precise schedule coordination — it only works with reliable, frequent inbound flows.
- Transportation economics explains why **hub-and-spoke networks** dominate both air and ocean shipping: consolidation at hubs achieves economies of scale that direct routes cannot.
