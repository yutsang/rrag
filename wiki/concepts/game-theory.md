## 概述

賽局理論（Game Theory）是研究多個理性行為人之間策略互動的數學框架，核心概念是納許均衡（Nash Equilibrium）：沒有任何參與者能透過單方面改變策略獲益的穩定策略組合。Nash（1950）定理保證每個有限賽局在混合策略下至少存在一個均衡。非合作賽局分為常式（Normal Form，一次性同時行動）與展開式（Extensive Form，序列行動樹），後者以逆向歸納法求子賽局完美均衡（SPNE），用於分析 Stackelberg 領導者-追隨者競爭及供應鏈中的先動優勢。合作賽局關注聯盟如何形成與分配剩餘，Shapley 值提供滿足效率、對稱、虛擬玩家及可加性四項公理的唯一公平分配方案，在機器學習特徵歸因（SHAP）與供應鏈成本分攤中均有應用。重複賽局的民俗定理（Folk Theorem）表明：當貼現因子足夠高時，長期關係中任何個別理性的可行報酬均可藉觸發策略維持為均衡，解釋了商業合作的持續性。在運籌學與管理科學研究中，賽局理論是供應鏈雙重邊際化（Double Marginalization）與合約協調、機制設計（VCG 拍賣）、競爭性收益管理及平台定價的理論基礎，與行為經濟學的交叉也揭示了均衡預測在實際決策中的局限。

## What It Is

**Game theory** models situations where the outcome for each agent depends on the actions of all agents. Unlike standard optimization (one decision-maker), games have multiple strategic actors whose choices are interdependent.

A **game** specifies: players $N = \{1, \ldots, n\}$; strategy sets $S_i$ for each player $i$; payoff functions $u_i: S_1 \times \cdots \times S_n \to \mathbb{R}$.

## Normal-Form Games

A **normal-form (strategic-form) game** lists all strategies and payoffs in a matrix (for two players).

**Example — Prisoner's Dilemma**:

|  | Cooperate | Defect |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooperate** | (3, 3) | (0, 5) |
| **Defect** | (5, 0) | (1, 1) |

- **Dominant strategy**: Defect dominates Cooperate for each player regardless of the other's action
- **Nash equilibrium**: (Defect, Defect) — neither player wants to unilaterally deviate
- **Pareto inefficiency**: (Cooperate, Cooperate) gives higher joint payoff but is not an equilibrium

This structure recurs in supply chains: each firm's incentive to defect on quality/lead-times leads to suboptimal industry outcomes.

## Nash Equilibrium

A strategy profile $(s_1^*, \ldots, s_n^*)$ is a **Nash equilibrium (NE)** if for each player $i$:
$$u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}^*) \geq u_i(s_i, s_{-i}^*) \quad \forall s_i \in S_i$$

No player has incentive to deviate unilaterally.

**Nash's theorem** (1950): every finite game has at least one NE in mixed strategies (where players randomize).

**Mixed strategy**: player $i$ chooses a probability distribution $\sigma_i \in \Delta(S_i)$ over pure strategies. In a mixed strategy NE, each player is indifferent between all strategies played with positive probability.

### Refinements
- **Dominant strategy equilibrium**: strategies are dominant (valid regardless of others' play)
- **Iterated elimination of dominated strategies (IESDS)**: iteratively remove dominated strategies
- **Subgame perfect Nash equilibrium (SPNE)**: NE in every subgame; found by backward induction

## Extensive-Form Games

**Extensive-form** represents sequential play as a game tree. Each node is a decision point; edges are actions; leaves are payoffs.

**Backward induction**: start from terminal nodes, work backward to find SPNE. Rational players anticipate future responses.

Example: **Stackelberg competition** — leader (firm 1) sets quantity first; follower (firm 2) best-responds. Leader gains first-mover advantage. Used to model supply chain leader-follower relationships.

**Centipede game, ultimatum game**: backward induction predictions often fail empirically — connects to [[behavioral-economics]].

## Cooperative Game Theory

**Cooperative games**: players can form coalitions and share payoffs. The question is how to divide the total surplus fairly.

**Characteristic function** $v(S)$: value generated by coalition $S$.

**Shapley value**: unique fair allocation satisfying axioms (efficiency, symmetry, dummy, additivity):
$$\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(|N|-|S|-1)!}{|N|!} [v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)]$$

Player $i$'s Shapley value = average marginal contribution across all orderings. Used in feature importance attribution in ML and cost allocation in supply chains.

**Core**: set of allocations no coalition would reject. May be empty.

## Key Applications in OR

### Supply Chain Contract Design
Double marginalization: when a manufacturer and retailer each optimize independently, total channel profit is below integrated optimum. Contracts (buyback, revenue sharing, quantity flexibility) can coordinate the channel to achieve first-best. Stackelberg game where manufacturer moves first.

### Auction and Mechanism Design
**Mechanism design** (reverse game theory): design the rules of the game so that strategic behavior leads to desired outcomes. Applications: spectrum auctions, procurement auctions, matching markets (hospitals-residents).

**Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) mechanism**: second-price auction is dominant-strategy truthful — bidding true valuation is optimal regardless of others. Used in online advertising.

### Oligopoly Models
**Cournot competition** (quantity): symmetric NE quantity is $q^* = (a - c)/(n+1)b$ per firm; more firms → closer to competitive outcome.

**Bertrand competition** (price): NE price = marginal cost even with two firms (Bertrand paradox); price wars in commodity markets.

**Hotelling location model**: spatial competition; explains product differentiation and retail location choices.

### Revenue Management with Competition
Airlines pricing in competitive markets: best-response pricing, price wars, tacit collusion in repeated games. See [[revenue-management]].

## Repeated Games

A **repeated game** plays a stage game $T$ times (finite or infinite). With sufficient patience (discount factor $\delta$ close to 1):

**Folk theorem**: any feasible and individually rational payoff can be sustained as a SPNE of the infinitely repeated game through trigger strategies (e.g., Grim trigger: cooperate unless defected, then punish forever).

Implication: cooperation is sustainable in ongoing business relationships even without contracts.
