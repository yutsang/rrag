## 概述

組織行為學（OB）是研究個人、群體及組織結構如何影響工作場所行為與績效的跨學科領域，整合心理學（動機、感知、態度）、社會學（群體動態、衝突、溝通）、人類學（組織文化）及經濟學（激勵設計、委託代理理論）的分析視角，在個人（人格、感知、動機）、群體（領導力、團隊、權力）與組織（文化、結構、變革）三個層次展開分析。個人層次的核心發現包括：Big Five 人格特質中責任心（Conscientiousness）對工作績效的預測效度最強；歸因理論區分內部（性格）vs外部（情境）歸因，並識別出「基本歸因錯誤」（過度歸因他人行為於性格）和「自我服務偏見」等認知偏差，這些偏差在[[wiki/concepts/behavioral-economics|行為經濟學]]中有更廣泛的理論闡釋。動機理論從內容（Maslow需求層次、Herzberg雙因素）到過程（Vroom期望理論：動機 = 期望 × 工具性 × 效價；Adams公平理論：不公平感觸發員工調整投入）再到強化（Skinner操作性條件反射）提供了多層次分析框架，期望理論與收益管理的激勵設計直接相連。領導力理論的演進路徑——特質理論→行為理論（俄亥俄州研究：主動結構vs關懷維度；Blake-Mouton管理方格）→情境理論（Hersey-Blanchard：領導風格須匹配部屬成熟度）→變革型vs交易型領導——揭示了情境適配是領導有效性的核心決定因素。組織文化（Schein的三層：人工製品→價值觀→基本假設）和組織變革（Lewin的解凍-變革-再凍結三步驟、Kotter的8步驟框架）是數位轉型與[[wiki/concepts/erp|ERP]]導入等大型企業變革的理論基礎，而抵制變革的心理根源（不確定性規避、損失厭惡、現狀偏見）同樣屬於行為經濟學的核心研究議題。

## What It Is

**Organizational Behavior (OB)** is the study of how individuals, groups, and organizational structures affect behavior within organizations, with the goal of improving organizational effectiveness.

Three levels of analysis:
1. **Individual**: personality, perception, attitudes, motivation, decision-making
2. **Group**: team dynamics, communication, conflict, leadership
3. **Organization**: culture, structure, change management, power

## Individual Level

### Personality
**Big Five (OCEAN)**: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Conscientiousness best predicts job performance across most occupations.

**Myers-Briggs (MBTI)**: popular in practice but limited scientific validity. Useful for team building/self-reflection, not selection.

**Core self-evaluations**: self-esteem, self-efficacy, locus of control, emotional stability. Predict job satisfaction and performance.

### Perception and Attribution
**Attribution theory**: do we attribute behavior to internal (dispositional) or external (situational) causes?
- **Fundamental attribution error**: over-attribute others' behavior to disposition, under-attribute to situation
- **Self-serving bias**: attribute own success to internal, failures to external

**Halo effect**: positive impression in one area colors judgments of unrelated areas. Affects performance appraisals.

### Attitudes and Job Satisfaction
**Job satisfaction** components: pay, promotion, supervision, co-workers, work itself.
**Organizational commitment**: affective (want to stay), normative (feel obligated), continuance (cost of leaving).
**Job satisfaction → performance**: correlation modest (~0.3). Dissatisfied employees are more likely to quit, have higher absenteeism.

## Motivation Theories

### Content Theories (what motivates)
**Maslow's hierarchy of needs**: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualization. Critique: people don't follow this rigid sequence.

**Herzberg's two-factor theory**: 
- **Hygiene factors** (pay, supervision, working conditions): prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate
- **Motivators** (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth): drive satisfaction and performance

**McClelland's acquired needs**: need for achievement (nAch), affiliation (nAff), power (nPow). High nAch individuals prefer moderate challenge, personal responsibility, and feedback.

### Process Theories (how motivation works)
**Expectancy theory (Vroom)**: Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence
- Expectancy: effort → performance (can I do it?)
- Instrumentality: performance → reward (will I get rewarded?)
- Valence: value of reward (do I want it?)

**Equity theory (Adams)**: compare own input/output ratio to a referent's. Inequity (over- or under-reward) creates tension motivating behavior to restore equity.

**Goal-setting theory (Locke)**: specific, challenging goals with feedback produce higher performance than vague ("do your best") goals. Basis for OKRs and MBO (Management by Objectives).

## Leadership

### Trait vs. Behavioral Theories
Early research sought universal leadership traits (intelligence, self-confidence, integrity). Ohio State studies identified two dimensions: **Initiating structure** (task focus) and **Consideration** (people focus). Both matter; high on both generally best.

### Contingency Theories
**Situational leadership (Hersey-Blanchard)**: match leadership style (directing, coaching, supporting, delegating) to follower's readiness (ability + willingness).

**Fiedler's contingency**: task-oriented leaders perform best in very favorable or unfavorable situations; relationship-oriented leaders best in moderate conditions.

### Transformational vs. Transactional
**Transactional leadership**: exchange — contingent rewards, management by exception. Effective for routine work.

**Transformational leadership**: inspire followers to transcend self-interest for collective goals. Components: idealized influence (charisma), inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration. Correlates with higher performance, creativity, and follower satisfaction.

## Group Dynamics and Teams

**Group development (Tuckman)**: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.

**Cohesion**: attraction among group members. High cohesion → strong performance if group norms align with organizational goals; can hurt if group norms support low performance (social loafing).

**Social loafing**: individuals exert less effort in groups than alone. Reduced by making contributions identifiable, increasing group size awareness, and linking individual contribution to group outcomes.

**Groupthink**: pressure for conformity suppresses dissent, leading to poor decisions. Prevention: devil's advocate, anonymous input, leader withholds opinion until discussion.

**Diverse teams**: better for complex, creative tasks; more conflict and communication challenges than homogeneous teams.

## Power and Conflict

**Sources of power** (French & Raven): legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent (charisma).

**Conflict types**: task conflict (what to do — often beneficial) vs. relationship conflict (personal animosity — usually harmful) vs. process conflict (how to do it).

**Conflict resolution**: competing, collaborating, compromising, accommodating, avoiding. Collaborating (win-win) is ideal when time allows.

## Connections to OR

OB is rarely mentioned in OR literature but shapes implementation success:
- **Change management**: implementing new OR systems requires managing resistance (Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze)
- **Decision-making biases**: [[behavioral-economics]] overlaps heavily with OB's individual decision-making section
- **Incentive design**: expectancy theory formalizes the mechanism behind principal-agent problems in [[game-theory]]
- **Team performance**: relevant for managing analytics teams and cross-functional project teams
